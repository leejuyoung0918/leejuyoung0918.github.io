Multiplayer VR · XR development internship at Overay

KAIST Live Concert

Another guest joined the concert, and my camera followed them.
In our multiplayer VR venue, each guest explored the stage through their own headset. During the second part of my internship at Overay, I worked on avatar movement and camera ownership, as well as the controls for changing the color of the stage lights.

Dates
Latter part of my Dec 2024 – Feb 2025 internship
My role
XR development intern · avatar synchronization, viewpoint tracking, stage lighting
Tools
Unity, C#, Normcore, Meta Avatar SDK

The moment a new avatar spawned

I compared the Unity object hierarchy just before and after a guest joined. The XR Origin, the reference for local headset tracking, was being attached as a child of the newly spawned avatar. I changed the attachment rule so only the avatar owned by the local player could receive the XR Origin.

Keeping head tilt and body rotation separate

I read the change in position and rotation from Camera Offset, applied the movement to the local avatar, and reset the offset. Only rotation about the vertical axis carried over to the avatar’s body, so looking up or tilting my head did not tip the entire avatar. Snap turns used the same frame of reference.

Why the light changed color but its beam stayed the same

Moving the stage color control changed the light source, but the visible beam kept its old color. The beam materials exposed different color properties depending on the shader. I checked which property each renderer supported, updated it, and tested the change on the headset. Both bugs taught me to trace who writes a value and what the receiving component actually reads.
