Mixed reality interaction · KAIST WIT Lab

EmbleMR

When someone says “translate this,” which object is “this”?
Someone can look away from an object before they finish speaking. At WIT Lab, we studied how a mixed reality interface connects an action to the intended object. I built the object-attached menu and voice controls for our comparison, then traced how targets changed during a task.

Dates
May – Aug 2026
My role
Undergraduate researcher · UI and voice input, user studies, interaction logs
Study
12 participants · 3 input methods · 7 tasks

Giving each input method the same tasks

The team studied how people could look at a physical object and use a hand gesture to request an action. I built the two comparison methods, an object-attached menu and voice input, and integrated seven tasks covering search, questions, translation, saving, anchoring, capture, and comparison. For search, translation, and comparison, we used prepared results to reduce variability in response generation.

Losing an object mid-sentence

When asking to compare two objects, a participant would often look from the first to the second while speaking. A selection based only on gaze at the end of the utterance could miss the first object. By aligning gaze trajectories with the target-selection and result timestamps, I could distinguish a selection error from a slow response.

Why the command mattered

Twelve participants tried all three input methods across seven tasks, with the order of the methods counterbalanced. I helped run the sessions, analyze errors, and prepare the manuscript. Voice was fastest when the task was to ask a question. When evaluating an input method, I now consider both how many objects someone needs to refer to and how naturally they can express the command.
