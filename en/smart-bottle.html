Smart Bottle

Reading water levels from a bottle cap to a phone
On a four-person team, I programmed the bottle’s Arduino and built its Bluetooth connection to an Android app. I also helped trace readings from the device through the app to the team’s server.

Dates
Spring 2025
My role
Hardware control, Android BLE integration, and integration testing
Tools
Arduino, HM-10, Kotlin, Jetpack Compose

From distance to volume

I mounted an ultrasonic sensor in the cap and used the echo’s travel time to calculate the distance to the water surface. Subtracting that distance from the empty-bottle reading gave the water height. The firmware used the bottle’s cross-sectional area to estimate the volume remaining and lit the LEDs according to the water level.

Making a missing reading visible

The sensor and phone could each work in isolation while no reading appeared on screen. I added a readout to the Android app so we could compare the string sent through the HM-10 module with the value the phone received. From there we checked the app’s request and the server’s response to locate the break in the chain.

Agreeing on what a reading means

Our team wrote the requirements and design documents and integrated the app with a FastAPI and MongoDB backend. Testing brought out a distinction that mattered across every interface. The amount left in a bottle and the amount a person drank describe different things. I learned to agree on what a value means, its unit, and where we can verify it before connecting the next component.
