Bayesian Preference Learning

How far apart should the options in the next question be?
Picking a favorite image can be easier than describing its features in numbers. I built an algorithm that learns from repeated choices and uses each answer to decide what to show next.

Dates
Jun 2026 – present
Role
Query design, estimation, simulation and user study design
Tools
Python · IDEAL-FINDER / NyangPick

When the options became too similar

Our first algorithm placed the options closer together as it narrowed down the user’s preference. Late in the search, even a little noise in a response left us with almost no information about what the user liked. I changed the design so that the uncertainty in the estimate and the spacing people could distinguish were separate quantities.

Building the next question

I found the direction in which the current estimate was least certain and placed several options along it. I implemented a rule for choosing their spacing based on the information an answer was expected to provide. For each round, I recorded the candidates, the estimated preference distribution and the random seed so methods could be compared under the same conditions.

Comparing 500 matched environments

I compared the methods across 500 synthetic environments. Choosing the spacing separately took fewer questions when the target precision was high. At intermediate precision, there was no clear saving. I also helped design the choice logs and evaluation measures for NyangPick, an interface in which participants pick the photos they prefer.
