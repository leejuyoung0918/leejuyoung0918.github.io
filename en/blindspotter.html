BlindSpotter

Predicting when a road user will emerge from a blind spot
I built graphs linking vehicles, pedestrians and occluded areas in a traffic scene, then tested how much past movement helped predict the appearance of pedestrians and e-scooter riders.

Period
March – June 2026
My role
Designed graph features, implemented models and evaluated their predictions
Tools & methods
Python, PyTorch, spatiotemporal graph neural networks

Representing a blind spot as a node

We studied situations such as a pedestrian emerging from behind a parked car or bus. Our team represented vehicles, vulnerable road users and the blind spots themselves as graph nodes. For each blind spot, the model predicted whether a road user would appear there in the near future.

Comparing one frame with five

I standardized the format of features such as position, distance, direction and relative speed. I capped extreme time-to-collision (TTC) values and applied a log transform. Because appearances were rare, I evaluated precision-recall and ROC curves as well as F1 across a range of decision thresholds.

What past movement added

In our team’s controlled experiment, we split the data by scene. The five-frame Social + ST-GCN model achieved an AUPRC of 0.4573, an AUROC of 0.8801 and a best F1 of 0.5481. EIGAT, which modeled spatial relationships in a single frame, reached an AUPRC of 0.3183. These scores measure how well the models predicted road-user appearances as defined in the project dataset.
