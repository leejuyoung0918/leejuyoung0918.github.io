Graph machine learning · Model development

BlindSpotter

Will someone appear from behind that vehicle?
When a bus blocks a driver’s view of an intersection, what can the last few frames tell us? Using IMPTC trajectories, I designed features describing how road users move around one another, then implemented and compared models that see one frame or five.

Period
March – June 2026
My role
Designed graph features, implemented models and evaluated their predictions
Tools & methods
Python, PyTorch, spatiotemporal graph neural networks

Trajectories from a German intersection

IMPTC is an open dataset collected with cameras and LiDAR at a single urban intersection in Germany. We read vehicle, pedestrian, cyclist and e-scooter tracks from sets 01–05. Matching positions and velocities by timestamp gave us a picture of who was in the intersection at each frame.

Drawing the area behind a vehicle

We used the vehicle with the longest track in a scene as the reference. From its viewpoint, we estimated the area hidden behind a vehicle such as a bus or truck as a polygon. Each hidden area became a graph node alongside the road users. Edges connected nodes within 30 metres of each other.

What counts as an appearance?

The public preprocessing code labels a blind spot positive if an e-scooter is observed inside its polygon or within six metres of its centre during the next three seconds. That rule lets us use future trajectories to train a model to anticipate an appearance near each blind spot.

When two objects barely close the gap

My edge features included distance, relative velocity, the difference in heading and estimated time to collision (TTC). When two objects approached each other very slowly, TTC could become extremely large. I limited it to 0–30 seconds and applied a log transform to reduce the influence of those values.

Keeping entire scenes together

The same people and vehicles appear in neighbouring frames. We split whole scenes into training, validation and test sets at 70%, 15% and 15%. About 1.2% of the test examples were positive, so we used area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC) to assess how often a model found those cases and how many false alarms it raised.

What the five-frame model picked up

The single-frame models reached test AUPRC scores of 0.3183 for EIGAT and 0.0147 for MR-GCN. The five-frame Social-STGCNN scored highest at 0.4573, with an AUROC of 0.8801 and a best F1 of 0.5481. All three models were evaluated on held-out scenes from the same intersection.
